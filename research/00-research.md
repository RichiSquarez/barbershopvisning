# Research — barbershop one-pagers, Bergen

Phase 1 of the brief: what the genre actually does, what Bergen gives a barbershop that nowhere else does,
and the ten concepts that follow from that.

---

## 1. Method

Three inputs, in this order of weight:

1. **The studied corpus.** The design skill's direction library is distilled from 247 gallery sites
   (One Page Love, Awwwards Sites of the Day) with a score attached to each. Everything below about
   "what scores" is read off that corpus, not off taste.
2. **The category.** What barbershop and small-hospitality sites in Norway actually put on a first
   screen: price, address, hours, booking, walk-in policy. Table stakes, not differentiation.
3. **The place.** Bergen is not a backdrop here. Timber, tar, rain, harbour, five walkable
   neighbourhoods with five different customer bases.

The rule taken from the corpus: **the pages that scored above 7.5 all took their device from the
subject's own material** — its name, its street, its constraint — while the pages that lost had a good
composition and a category idea. So the ten concepts below are ranked by *what only a Bergen barbershop
has*, not by which layout is nicest.

---

## 2. Ten patterns that recur on high-scoring pages

These are the moves that repeat across the corpus; each page in this build uses one as its spine.

| # | Pattern | What it looks like | Why it scores |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The name as architecture** | The wordmark set at 14–19vw, cropped by an edge, or stacked to fill the first screen; everything else small | The name is the only asset a small shop owns outright. Most common device among 7.5+ sites |
| 2 | **One dark field** | A single near-black ground for the whole page, photographs lit out of it like exhibits, no section bands | Cheap to do well, expensive to fake; reads as a room rather than a document |
| 3 | **The split stage** | Two panels, one scrolling, one pinned (photograph or price board) | Turns a one-pager into a barbershop window: you watch one thing while reading another |
| 4 | **The pinned frame** | A single frame holds its place while its contents change as you scroll | Says "this is a series" (cuts, people, prices) without a carousel |
| 5 | **The place carries it** | The neighbourhood named in the hero, its photograph inside the sentence | Local trust: the address is the argument |
| 6 | **One motif repeated** | A single drawn/printed element (awning stripe, folder tab, ticket) used as band, bullet, hover state | Makes ten sections read as one shop |
| 7 | **Loud flat colour** | Full-bleed saturated field, two inks, headline nearly touching itself | The category's default is quiet beige; the opposite is remembered |
| 8 | **The page as object** | The layout imitates a physical thing: a folder stack, a menu sheet, a coupon, a ticket | Gives the visitor something to handle; unusual at this price point |
| 9 | **Type and nothing else** | No photograph in the first screen; two inks, one enormous two-line statement | Fastest page to load, and the strongest statement of confidence |
| 10 | **Dressed as something else** | A barbershop laid out like a fashion drop, a farm notice, a record sleeve | The contrast between dull trade and vivid dress is what is remembered |

Two anti-patterns confirmed by the same corpus, worth naming because the category is full of them:
the **centred headline over three lines with a small label above it**, and the **row of three icon
cards**. Neither appears in this build.

## 3. Category table stakes (must be on every page, somewhere)

Not differentiation — the things whose absence makes a real visitor leave.

- **Price**, in NOK, per service, visible without opening a PDF.
- **The CTA.** "Bestill time" as the primary verb. "Drop-in" as the honest fallback where the shop
  takes walk-ins; several Bergen shops do, and saying so is a selling point.
- **Address + neighbourhood.** Bergen people navigate by neighbourhood, not by postcode.
- **Opening hours**, including the Saturday half-day (most Norwegian barbershops close ~15:00 Sat and
  all day Sunday).
- **Phone** as a `tel:` link. Norwegian customers still call.
- **Cancellation / no-show terms** at least as one line. Norwegian shops commonly say 24 hours.
- **Credits** for the found photography, small, in the footer.

Stock services and Bergen-plausible prices used across the ten pages:

## 4. Bergen inventory

What only this city gives the subject. Each of the ten pages is built on one line of this table.

**Neighbourhoods, and who they serve**

| Place | Character | Customer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bryggen | 1700s timber warehouses on the wharf, tarred and leaning, UNESCO | Tourists, harbour workers, people who want the postcard |
| Nordnes | Small peninsula, white wooden houses, the aquarium, locals' streets | Neighbours, walk-ins, fixed prices on a painted board |
| Sandviken | Old white timber houses up the hillside, shipyard history | Men who keep a beard for years and want it done slowly |
| Åsane | Suburb, shopping centre, families, three generations in one block | Fathers, sons, schoolboys |
| Nygårdstangen / sentrum | Student Bergen, bus station, cheap rents | Students, young men, first flat |
| Bergen waterfront / containers | Kai — quay. Steel, rope, salt, roller doors | Harbour crew, shift workers, industrial trade |
| Fyllingsdalen / Laksevåg | West-side suburb, families, bybanen | Families, commuters after 16:00 |

**Weather and light.** 210–230 rain days a year. Wet hair is a real condition, not a metaphor. Winter
light is blue for four hours; summer light lasts to 23:00. Both are usable as a page's ground colour.

**Printed things of the trade.** The price board painted on a board (Nordnes), the appointment slip,
the clip-card, the exam timetable at a barber school, the awning stripe, the harbour container's
stencilled numbers.

**Trade history, usable as real imagery.** Barber-surgeon engravings (Abraham Bosse, 1632), the
razor-and-strop still life, the shaving set, the sheep-shearing paintings (this is where the trade's
tools come from), Haakonshallen's timber interior, old maps of the harbour.

**The school angle.** Bergen has vocational barber training: students cut at reduced price under a
master, with a timetable, a practical exam, and an apprenticeship afterwards. That is a whole page with
no competitor equivalent in the city.

## 5. The ten pages

Each row: the concept, the one sentence the page exists to prove, the device, and the material it is
built from. Full sheets are in each folder's `DIRECTION.md`.

| Page | Direction | Idea sentence | Built from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 Bryggen Barber | The dark room | On this page, **harbour light at night** becomes the only light source: the shop is a dim room and every photograph is a lit exhibit | Bryggen's floodlit timber at dusk |
| 02 Regn & Barber | The name as a monument | On this page, **210 rain days a year** becomes the house style: one wet city, one dry room | Bergen's rain, as a stated fact and a photograph |
| 03 Nordnes Barber | The split stage | On this page, **the painted price board** becomes a fixed panel, while the street moves past it | Walk-in only, five chairs, prices on a board |
| 04 Fjell & Fade | The pinned poster | On this page, **the student price** becomes the thing that stays put while everything else changes | Cheap fades for students near Nygårdstangen |
| 05 Sandviken Skjegg | The place carries it | On this page, **the white wooden house** becomes the sentence itself, a photograph standing where a word should be | Slow beard work in an old hillside house |
| 06 Barberskolen | One motif | On this page, **the school's awning stripe** becomes the structure: it rules the card, the bullets and the buttons | Vocational training, students, exam timetable |
| 07 Kai 12 | Loud and flat | On this page, **the container's stencilled paint** becomes the whole page: four saturated fields, two inks, no photograph in the first screen | A barbershop in a quay container |
| 08 Åsane Barber | The page is the object | On this page, **the family photo drawer** becomes the navigation: five folders, each one a section | Three generations in a shopping centre |
| 09 Nattbarber | Type and nothing else | On this page, **the hour** becomes the page: a sky-blue field at 18:00, a navy one at 01:00 | Open 18:00–01:00 for shift and restaurant staff |
| 10 Sjø & Saks | Dressed as something else | On this page, **sheep shearing** becomes the category the shop dresses as, down to the printed notice and the coupon | A shop that cuts whole families, kids free under ten |

No two pages share a direction, a typeface, a palette, a hero composition or a section order. Checked
against the dealt sheets: 10/10 unique on all five counts.

## 6. Non-negotiable constraints for the build

- Norwegian Bokmål copy, `du`-form, sentence case. No English UI words except trade terms that
  Norwegians actually use ("fade", "drop-in").
- Headline ≤ 2 lines at every width including 390px.
- Body ≥ 16px; nothing below 13px.
- At least four real photographs per page (floor checked in a real browser), credits in the footer,
  never presented as the shop's own team or premises.
- One button shape, one corner radius, one margin pair per page.
- No gradients, no shadows at rest, no monospace, no numbered labels, no thin decorative rules,
  no icon cards, no emoji.
- `lint.mjs` clean, `inspect.mjs` clean at 1440 / 768 / 390, screenshots looked at.
